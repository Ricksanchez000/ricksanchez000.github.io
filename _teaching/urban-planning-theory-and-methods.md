Rapid urbanisation has made urban planning theory and methods increasingly important. This course provides students with a rigorous understanding of urban planning and the social and cultural forces that shape planning thought and practice.

## Course Outline

### Week 1: Core Concepts in Urban and Rural Planning and the Urbanisation Process (3 contact hours)

- Fundamental definitions and characteristics of urban and rural areas
- Global urbanisation trends and their impacts
- Challenges and opportunities associated with urbanisation in China

### Week 2: Urban Geography and Urban Sociology (3 contact hours)

- Foundational concepts and the scope of urban geography
- The relationship between urban sociology and urban development
- Intersections among urban space, power and identity

### Week 3: Qualitative Research and Planning (3 contact hours)

- Introduction to qualitative research methods and examples of their application
- The value of in-depth interviews and fieldwork in planning
- Deriving planning insights from qualitative data

### Week 4: Mixed-Methods Research Design (3 contact hours)

- Characteristics of, and distinctions between, qualitative and quantitative research
- Approaches to integrating qualitative and quantitative methods
- Applying mixed-methods research in planning projects

### Week 5: Public Participation and Community Planning (3 contact hours)

- Definitions, forms and methods of public participation
- Case studies in community planning
- Differences and challenges in participation across urban and rural communities

### Week 6: Transport Planning and Human Behaviour (3 contact hours)

- An introduction to qualitative approaches to transport planning
- Interconnections among commuting, leisure and everyday life
- The relationship between transport and quality of life in urban and rural areas

### Week 7: Group Project Presentations: Urban Studies (3 contact hours)

Student groups present research projects on selected topics in urban studies.

### Week 8: Applications of Cultural and Social Geography in Planning (3 contact hours)

- Foundational concepts in cultural geography and their significance for planning
- Analysing urban–rural relations through the lens of social geography
- Relationships among space, power and identity in urban and rural contexts

### Week 9: Rural Geography and Rural Sociology (3 contact hours)

- Foundational concepts and research methods in rural geography
- The relationship between rural sociology and rural development
- Interconnections among rural space, culture and society

### Week 10: Rural Planning and Rural Revitalisation (3 contact hours)

- Traditional values and contemporary challenges in rural areas
- Rural planning methods and strategies
- The relationship between rural tourism and rural revitalisation

### Week 11: Public Space and Public Life (3 contact hours)

- Definitions, types and design principles of public space
- Differences and connections between urban and rural public spaces
- Public participation in the design and use of public space

### Week 12: Urban Governance (3 contact hours)

- Definitions, characteristics and approaches to urban governance
- Urban governance case studies from local to global scales
- Participation, collaboration and conflict in urban governance

### Week 13: Urban Regeneration and Old-City Renewal (3 contact hours)

- Definitions, methods and challenges of old-city renewal
- The cultural and social value of old-city renewal
- Applications of qualitative research in urban renewal projects

### Week 14: Urban–Rural Interaction and Complementarity (3 contact hours)

- The urban–rural dual structure in China and abroad: historical evolution and contemporary conditions
- Challenges and opportunities in urban–rural relations
- Strategies and methods for strengthening urban–rural complementarity

### Week 15: Group Project Presentations: Rural Studies (3 contact hours)

Student groups present research projects on selected topics in rural studies.

### Week 16: Course Review and Future Directions (3 contact hours)

- Review of the concepts and methods covered during the semester
- Future trends and directions in urban and rural planning
- Student feedback and instructor guidance
